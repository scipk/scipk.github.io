GLACIER LAB · 2026– · RENDEZVOUS CONTROL · MATLAB/SIMULINK

Robust Rendezvous Control

GLACIER Lab, University of California, Los Angeles. M.S. research under Professor Shahriar Talebi, 2026–.

MATLAB/SIMULINKROBUST CONTROLORBITAL MECHANICSRENDEZVOUS

Problem

A chaser spacecraft closing on a target has to drive relative position and relative velocity to zero using actuators whose behaviour is never known exactly. The linearized models this is normally designed against — the Clohessy–Wiltshire form of Hill’s equations — are exact only for a circular reference orbit and for separations small compared with the orbit radius.

That gap is the hard part. Mass properties shift as propellant is spent, thrust is never perfectly aligned, the reference orbit is not exactly circular, and the navigation solution feeding the controller carries its own error. A controller tuned to the nominal plant can lose the margin it was designed with by the time the true plant has moved. The problem I am working on is designing rendezvous control whose stability and performance hold across that uncertainty set, rather than holding only at the nominal model.

FIGURE · LVLH GEOMETRY, CHASER AND TARGET

Approach

Relative motion is written in the target’s LVLH frame, where the in-plane radial and along-track axes couple through the mean motion and the cross-track axis decouples into a simple oscillator. Uncertainty is carried explicitly in the plant description rather than absorbed into hand-tuned margin, so that what the synthesis returns is a guarantee over a set of plants and not over one. The design work runs in MATLAB and Simulink.

MODELLinearized relative motion in the LVLH frame from the target’s circular reference orbit.
UNCERTAINTYBound the deviation between the true and nominal plant: mass properties, actuator alignment, reference-orbit eccentricity, navigation error.
SYNTHESISDesign the controller against the bounded set, then check the closed loop against the nonlinear dynamics.
